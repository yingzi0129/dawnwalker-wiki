Bakir is the second of the three Boyars in The Blood of Dawnwalker — the Mad Khan, game master of the vampire court''s games — and his boss fight is called **The Lunar Game**. Where the [Ambrus duel](/bosses/ambrus/) is a private contest of honor, Bakir turns his death into a public spectacle: you kill him **during the games**, in front of the court that cheered for him. This guide covers how the Lunar Game unlocks, what kind of fight to expect, and the strategy that carries it. A transparency note: Bakir''s exact attack data is still being documented by the community in the launch window, so this page sticks to what is confirmed and flags the rest.

![Coen battling Bakir in a dark dungeon fight in The Blood of Dawnwalker](/images/articles/bakir.webp)

## How the Lunar Game Unlocks

The same court system that gates Ambrus gates Bakir: work through **his** [court activities](/guides/court-activities/) — his chains of prisoners, collectors, transports, and dungeons across the regions — until his anger meter in the Court menu hits "Angered." His boss quest then appears on the map. There is no way to rush this gate; roughly three-quarters of his activities is the threshold that worked for Ambrus, and Bakir follows the same pattern.

Plan your time. Every court activity spends time segments, and the whole story still runs on the 30-day limit — but a focused campaign against one Boyar costs only a few days, so the pressure is manageable. Bank the skill points each activity pays out; the games are a difficulty spike, not a formality.

## What Kind of Fight the Lunar Game Is

Confirmed by the encounter''s framing: this is an arena spectacle, not a private duel. The court watches, the games have an audience, and the encounter is built around pressure from more than one direction. Treat anything that joins the fight as a mechanic rather than background noise — the fastest way to lose the Lunar Game is to watch only Bakir''s health bar while everything else wears you down.

The reliable combat tools are the same ones the whole game runs on, verified from the harder duels around it: **Perfect Block into Perfect Riposte** for your core damage, dodge — never trade — against heavy committed swings, and lock-on so nothing repositions behind you for free. If Bakir follows Ambrus''s design language, expect unblockable strings with visible tells and at least one escalation phase at low health; the community is confirming the specifics, and this section will be updated as they are verified.

## General Strategy for the Games

Until Bakir''s exact strings are mapped, the strategy that wins spectacle fights in The Blood of Dawnwalker is positioning discipline:

- **Fight at the edges, not the center.** Arena centers are where interference converges. Keeping your back to open space lets you see every threat at once.
- **Clear interference on a schedule.** Decide in advance: whenever the field gets crowded, you spend a few seconds cleaning it before re-engaging Bakir. Reactive clearing always costs more health than scheduled clearing.
- **Dodge first, punish second.** Every boss in The Blood of Dawnwalker punishes greed harder than caution. One confirmed safe hit after a dodge, then reset to neutral.
- **Spend before you arrive.** Skill points unspent are damage not dealt. Enter the games with your build finished, healing stocked, and a fresh manual save — 54 slots exist, use one here.

## Is the Beast of Balaur Part of the Games?

Community reports connect the [Beast of Balaur](/bosses/beast-of-balaur/) encounter to Bakir''s storyline — the beast is fought in the same stretch of the campaign as the games, and players searching for how to kill Bakir are usually pointed at the same region. We are verifying exactly where the Beast of Balaur sits relative to the Lunar Game before stating it as fact; treat the connection as likely rather than confirmed. In the meantime, our Beast of Balaur guide covers that fight on its own page, and the [endgame guide](/guides/endgame/) explains why dealing with every Boyar before the final quest matters — undealt-with Boyars reappear as minibosses inside Greifberg Castle.

## What You Get for Winning

Killing Bakir during the games removes the second Boyar from the board and pays out the same structure as the other duels: XP, a skill point, and his equipment. More strategically, it brings you one step from maximum Infamy — and maximum Infamy is what forces Brencis to summon you for the final quest. Two Boyars down means the endgame is one campaign away; the [main quest order](/guides/main-quests/) shows what remains.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Lunar Game in The Blood of Dawnwalker?**
The boss quest of Bakir, the Mad Khan — the second of the three Boyars. You unlock it by completing enough of his court activities to anger him, and in it you kill him during the games he hosts. The trophy for it reads: "Kill Bakir during the games. Vale Sangora will need a new game master."

**How do I unlock the Bakir fight?**
Through his court activities. Open the Court menu, watch his anger meter, and keep clearing his activity chains across the regions until it maxes out. His quest then appears on the map like Ambrus''s did.

**Who is the Mad Khan?**
Bakir''s title in the court of The Blood of Dawnwalker. He is the game master — the Boyar who runs the games — which is why his death is itself one of them.

**Is the Beast of Balaur the same fight as Bakir?**
Probably related, not yet confirmed. The Beast of Balaur is fought in the same campaign stretch; whether it is part of the Lunar Game, a separate encounter, or Bakir''s own beast form is exactly what the community is documenting right now. This page updates when it is verified.

**Can I make the Bakir fight easier?**
Indirectly, yes: finish your build with the skill points his activities pay, clear interference instead of tanking it, and save before starting the quest so a loss costs minutes, not progress. There is no confirmed poison-style shortcut for Bakir the way Ambrus has The Bittersweet Toast — if one is found, it will be added here.
