If the main story of The Blood of Dawnwalker is the skeleton, court activities are the muscle. They look like optional flavor — aristocratic games run by the courts of 14th-century Europe — but they are a core progression system in The Blood of Dawnwalker, woven directly into the [main quest order](/guides/main-quests/) between major story beats. This guide explains what each confirmed court activity is, when you unlock it, and why skipping them makes the rest of The Blood of Dawnwalker harder than it needs to be.

## What Court Activities Are in The Blood of Dawnwalker

Court activities are a set of structured challenges hosted by the game's noble factions. Three are confirmed at launch:

| Activity | Type | Introduced | Progression Value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Gilded Gauntlet | Combat trial | After the Ambrus storyline | Skill points, gear |
| The Lunar Game | Stealth/night challenge | Act II court stretch | Skill points, rare resources |
| The Ring | Duel arena | Act II court stretch | Skill points, unique rewards |

The Blood of Dawnwalker uses these activities as pacing valves: after intense story stretches (the duel with [Ambrus](/bosses/ambrus/), the games surrounding [Bakir](/bosses/bakir/)), the game deliberately routes you into the courts. Treat that routing as the developers telling you where the difficulty curve expects your build to be.

## The Gilded Gauntlet

The Gilded Gauntlet is the first major court activity most players meet in The Blood of Dawnwalker, and it is the most straightforward: a staged combat trial where you fight through escalating waves under arena conditions. Think of it as the game's report card on everything the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) tells you to practice — dodge timing, parry windows, and target priority.

**How to approach it:** arrive with healing items, spend any hoarded skill points first, and do not be proud about retrying rounds. The Gauntlet in The Blood of Dawnwalker rewards clean play, not desperate aggression; most failed runs end because players get greedy after a perfect parry. The skill points it pays out make it the single most efficient early progression boost in The Blood of Dawnwalker.

## The Lunar Game

The Lunar Game is where The Blood of Dawnwalker leans into its identity. Run at night and built around your vampire side, it plays with stealth, movement, and the supernatural toolkit that the day/night cycle normally gates. If you have been treating night as the dangerous half of The Blood of Dawnwalker, the Lunar Game is the game teaching you to treat it as your home turf.

**How to approach it:** before attempting the Lunar Game, get comfortable operating at night in the open world — clear a few night-time activities, learn which enemies you can bypass rather than fight, and make sure your loadout favors mobility. The Lunar Game punishes the same habit that ruins night exploration elsewhere in The Blood of Dawnwalker: trying to fight through everything instead of choosing engagements.

## The Ring

The Ring is the duel arena of the courts — and the court activity most directly connected to the boss skillset of The Blood of Dawnwalker. Where the Gauntlet tests endurance and the Lunar Game tests stealth, the Ring tests pure one-on-one combat discipline: read, dodge, punish, repeat.

**How to approach it:** the Ring is the best practice space in The Blood of Dawnwalker for the fights that actually gate your progress. If a [boss guide](/bosses/) on this site mentions a pattern you are struggling to read, the Ring is where you drill it without story stakes.

## Where to Find Court Activities

Court activities have dedicated locations across the world of The Blood of Dawnwalker, marked on your map as you discover them — typically tied to the noble estates and game grounds of the regions you pass through during the story. If you are in an act's court stretch and do not see activity markers, push the surrounding story quests a little further; the game gates each court's activities behind narrative progress rather than hiding them in obscure corners.

## Why You Should Not Skip Them

Three reasons court activities are effectively mandatory in The Blood of Dawnwalker:

1. **Skill points.** The progression currency you need for ultimate perks flows generously from court activities — skipping them means arriving at late-game fights under-leveled.
2. **Difficulty pacing.** The Blood of Dawnwalker tunes its [main quests](/guides/main-quests/) assuming you have done the court content of each act.
3. **Unlock structure.** Some court activities feed quest outcomes, and the games surrounding Bakir in particular blur the line between court activity and main story.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are court activities missable in The Blood of Dawnwalker?**
The activities themselves remain available, but their ideal difficulty window is when they unlock. Doing the Gauntlet two acts late in The Blood of Dawnwalker is technically fine and practically boring.

**Do court activities have difficulty options?**
The Blood of Dawnwalker scales combat challenges with its global difficulty setting, so lower it if a trial is blocking your story progress — see the [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide/) for why that is a legitimate strategy.

**What is the best court activity to do first?**
The Gilded Gauntlet. It unlocks first, pays skill points immediately, and every point it gives compounds through the rest of The Blood of Dawnwalker.
