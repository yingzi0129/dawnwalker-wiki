Court activities are the engine of the mid-game in The Blood of Dawnwalker. The three Boyars — [Ambrus](/bosses/ambrus/), Bakir, and Xanthe — each run a network of repeatable missions across the regions, and working through those networks is how you force each Boyar into a duel you can actually win. This guide explains how court activities work, what they cost, and how they feed the story.

![Four vampire lords of the court gathered in candlelight in The Blood of Dawnwalker](/images/articles/court-activities.webp)

## How Court Activities Work in The Blood of Dawnwalker

Each Boyar hands out activity chains in the regions around their territory. Ambrus, for example, posts chains such as Ambrus''s Prisoners, Ambrus''s Collectors, Ambrus''s Transports, Ambrus''s Dungeons, and Ambrus''s Cellars, plus named side quests like Shadows in the Woods. Each Boyar has roughly seven activities in circulation, and every one you complete does two things:

1. **It angers the Boyar.** The Boyar''s anger is shown on their icon in the Court menu. Around three-quarters of their activities is enough to push a Boyar to "Angered" — Ambrus cracks at about 75%.
2. **It raises your Infamy.** Infamy is the court-wide wanted system of The Blood of Dawnwalker, capped at level 9. Each level applies a negative Edict: more enemy patrols on the roads, higher prices at vendors, and a world that generally hates you more.

Anger is personal; Infamy is political. You need both: anger unlocks each duel, and maximum Infamy is what finally forces Brencis to summon you for the final quest.

## The Three Boss Duels Court Activities Unlock

When a Boyar hits maximum anger, their boss fight quest appears on the map. These are the three duels the whole system builds toward:

| Boyar | Boss Quest | What Happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ambrus | **The Gilded Gauntlet** | A formal duel at night. An optional poison plot (The Bittersweet Toast) can weaken him first. |
| Bakir | **The Lunar Game** | Bakir — the Mad Khan, game master of the courts — is killed during his own games. |
| Xanthe | **The Cycle of Love** | The one duel you can avoid: kill Xanthe, or make a deal with her instead. |

The [Ambrus boss guide](/bosses/ambrus/) covers the Gilded Gauntlet duel phase by phase, and the [Bakir guide](/bosses/bakir/) covers the Lunar Game. Xanthe''s duel is the exception to the rule: a deal with her counts as neutralizing her threat, and it changes the final assault on Greifberg Castle in your favor.

## What Court Activities Cost: Time Segments

Everything in The Blood of Dawnwalker spends time. Each day is divided into 16 time segments — eight by day, eight by night — and quests, travel, training, and dialogue all deduct segments like a currency. Court activities each advance the clock a little, which matters because the whole story runs on a 30-day limit to save Coen''s family. Spend segments deliberately: the focused main path finishes in under 10 in-game days, so the system is generous — but only if you do not waste evenings standing around.

Some court activities are also **night-gated**. The Gilded Gauntlet duel itself can only be started at night: you ring a bell to summon Ambrus, which costs two time segments, and ringing before sundown simply gives you the option to wait for nightfall.

## Court Activities and Infamy: The Double-Edged Design

Court activities are not free progression. Every completed activity pushes your Infamy up, and each Infamy level makes the open world worse for you — this is deliberate. The Blood of Dawnwalker wants you to feel the courts closing in as you dismantle them. Practical consequences you will notice:

- **Road travel gets dangerous.** Patrol density rises with Infamy, so plan routes between activities instead of wandering.
- **Shopping gets expensive.** Vendor price penalties stack per level; buy gear and consumables early, before your Infamy climbs.
- **The endgame trigger depends on it.** Only at maximum Infamy does the Edict cutscene play and Brencis''s parley message arrive. There is no way to reach the ending quests without angering the whole court — embrace it.

## A Sensible Order for the Court Phase

You can tackle the three Boyars in any order, and their activity chains overlap geographically. An efficient rhythm most players settle into:

1. **Clear activities in clusters by region**, not by Boyar — the map already routes you through shared areas, and batching saves travel time.
2. **Watch the Court menu, not the journal.** The Boyar anger meters live in the Court menu; when one flashes "Angered," detour to their duel immediately. Duels are the progression spike, not the activities.
3. **Bank skill points before each duel.** Each activity chain pays skill points, and the duels assume you spent them. Arriving at the Gilded Gauntlet with unspent points is the most common self-inflicted loss in The Blood of Dawnwalker.
4. **Save before and after every duel.** The duels are the difficulty peaks of the mid-game; a quick manual save converts a bad attempt into a two-minute retry.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many court activities does each Boyar have?**
Roughly seven per Boyar, spread across the regions. You do not need all of them — about 75% is enough to trigger the angered state and unlock the duel.

**Do court activities expire?**
No. Side content in The Blood of Dawnwalker does not fail from time passing; only the overall 30-day family limit is on a clock. Take the activities at your own pace, but do not take forever.

**Can I skip court activities entirely?**
No. The three boss duels are gated behind them, and the duels are gated behind the story. Court activities are the main quest wearing a side-quest costume.

**What happens at maximum Infamy?**
Skip time to the next day. The Infamy Edicts cutscene plays, Brencis demands a parley, and the final quest A Deal with the Devil opens — the door to every ending. The [endgame guide](/guides/endgame/) covers what to finish before you walk through it.
