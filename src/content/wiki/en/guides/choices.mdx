Every marketing line about The Blood of Dawnwalker mentions the same promise: a story shaped by your actions. This guide explains what that actually means in practice — which choices genuinely change the game, how consequences travel through the story, and one design truth about the [endings](/guides/endings/) that most players never see coming.

![Coen facing a hooded figure in a candlelit confrontation in The Blood of Dawnwalker](/images/articles/choices.webp)

## The One Design Truth to Internalize First

Here is the spoiler-free headline: **your earlier choices shape the journey; the ending is decided almost entirely at the end.** Only two things decide which of the seven endings you get:

1. **Which ally questlines you finished** — Lacra's chain and Crake's Manumit chain (full lists in the [quest list](/guides/all-quests/)).
2. **Your dialogue choices during the final quest**, A Deal with the Devil.

Everything else — mercy calls, secrets, loyalties, Esme's medicine — changes who lives, who trusts you, and what the world looks like when you arrive at the final quest. It rarely locks an ending. That is genuinely good news: it means you can play the story on instinct and still keep every ending reachable with one well-placed save.

## Choices That Genuinely Change the Game

### Who Stands Beside You

The most mechanically significant choices in The Blood of Dawnwalker are the ally questlines. Finish Lacra's chain and she fights [Brencis](/bosses/brencis/) with you; finish Crake's and the Manumit join you; finish neither and you face the final boss alone. The same choice also decides whether the betrayal route is even open, because The Turncoat requires having completed three Manumit quests first.

### Kill or Deal: Xanthe and the Final Assault

The [Xanthe decision](/bosses/xanthe/) is the game's cleanest example of a mid-game choice reshaping the endgame: kill her and the threat is gone, or take her deal and she repays it during the final assault on Greifberg Castle, changing the final dungeon in your favor. Neither locks an ending — it changes how the last hours play.

### Esme's Medicine

The prologue's crafting puzzle — hot water and three spoonfuls of herbs for your sick mother — echoes further than anything else that early. Get it right and she survives; get it wrong and she cannot be part of the family rescue. Two minutes of attention in the tutorial, permanent consequences thirty days later.

### Ambrus: Poison, Blood, and  Letting Him In

The [Ambrus duel](/bosses/ambrus/) is a nest of consequential choices: whether you run Iulian Codrin's poison plot, what you say at the toast (which decides whether Ambrus is fully poisoned, both of you are, or Iulian dies), and after the duel, whether you drain Ambrus or kill him — and whether you let him in. Each answer changes rewards, relationships, and what the court thinks of you.

## Choices That Matter Less Than They Look

- **Boyar order.** The three campaigns can be done in any sequence; the map barely cares. Completeness matters, order does not.
- **Most dialogue.** Weighted conversations feel momentous, but unless they sit in an ally questline or the final quest, they usually alter flavor and world state, not your fate.
- **Efficiency anxiety.** With 16 time segments a day and a 30-day limit, players obsess over wasting time. A focused main path takes under 10 in-game days — the clock pressures dawdlers, not explorers.

## How to Protect Yourself From Bad Outcomes

You cannot make every choice correctly — the game is built so that wanting two incompatible things is normal. What you can do is keep your options open:

- **Manual save before every major quest.** Autosaves will not protect a branch you regret, and the game offers 54 manual slots — use them.
- **Save before long dialogue scenes.** Multiple named characters arguing in one room is a decision node; save first.
- **Do Lacra's chain early and in order.** Her hidden quest A Mother's Plea is missable and only triggers in sequence.
- **Make a master save before A Deal with the Devil.** One fresh slot, never overwritten — it is the key to every full ending from a single playthrough.

## A Sensible First-Run Strategy

Community consensus for a first playthrough: **play what you believe, save often, and let the first ending be yours.** Chasing an optimal route on run one turns the game's best feature into a checklist. Use the [endings guide](/guides/endings/) and this page on your second run, when you are engineering branches instead of discovering them.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I miss content permanently?**
Yes — A Mother's Plea, some quest-giver branches, and character outcomes close as the story advances. Manual saves are your safety net; side quests themselves never expire to the clock.

**Do choices affect combat, not just story?**
Yes. The three fight versions of the [Brencis confrontation](/bosses/brencis/) prove the game rewrites encounters around your decisions, and the Xanthe deal changes the final dungeon itself.

**Is there a way to see all consequences in one playthrough?**
Not organically — but one master save before the final quest unlocks every full ending, which covers the endings most players care about. The early endings are separate triggers best seen from earlier saves.
